Abstract

Video editing is increasingly evolving toward longer durations and more precise object-level manipulation, yet existing benchmarks remain insufficient for evaluating these emerging capabilities. We introduce LOVE-Bench, comprising 650 source videos averaging 43 seconds, 1,020 editing instructions, and 220 reference images across four object-level editing tasks. Fine-grained spatial and temporal annotations enable comprehensive evaluation of long-horizon stability, source fidelity, instruction compliance, and video quality. We further introduce LOVE-Stream, a training-free streaming editing pipeline based on the Wan2.1 T2V model that employs halo-based source-context conditioning to mitigate temporal discontinuities across video chunks. Using LOVE-Bench, we benchmark existing video editing methods, demonstrating its ability to provide comprehensive evaluations that closely align with human judgments. Our results reveal that existing methods often struggle to maintain temporally consistent object-level edits over extended durations, while LOVE-Stream achieves improved temporal consistency and source fidelity.

03 / EVALUATION

Beyond short-term quality.

Four complementary tests for edits that need to last.

CPCompliance persistence+
Combines mean compliance, the weakest observations, and early-to-late decline.CP = (c̄ · Lq(c) · [1 − max(0, c̄early − c̄late)])1/3

Does the requested edit persist over time?

BHCBeyond-horizon consistency+
Compares consistency at temporal separations H and 4H; penalizes long-range drift.BHC = √(Cfar · [1 − max(0, Cnear − Cfar)])

Do distant observations still agree?

AOSAbrupt object shift+
Penalizes large and frequent feature jumps between adjacent windows. Annotated cuts and occlusions are excluded.AOS = √((1 − J95) · (1 − Rτ))

Does the object suddenly shift or flicker?

PORRPost-occlusion recovery+
For eligible reappearance events, measures post-event quality, recovery relative to pre-event quality, and time to sustained recovery.PORRquality = (Qpost · min(1, Qpost / Qpre) · Trec)1/3

Does the edit return when the object does?
